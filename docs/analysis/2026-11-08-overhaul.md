# Neon Dash overhaul analysis — 8 Nov 2026

## What the owner and the reviewer saw

- **Vertical transition:** "no animation nor explanation and becomes a sudden death wall."
- **Look:** "Neon look and feel aren't there"; the shaft had dead dark bars on both sides on phones.
- **Fun:** a casual player died within about 5 seconds (6% in the opening clip, 4% at a gap in the transition clip). The first ten seconds were not fun.
- **Text:** the menu was six lines of rules; the shaft hint ("CYAN ← VIOLET → SWITCH TO THE OPEN SIDE") was tiny and overlapped the switch button; the game-over card had a seed, achievements, three buttons and tiny text.

## How it was measured

Everything below comes from the real simulation (`core.ts`), not a model of it.

- **Ghost-input bots** (`src/autoplay.ts`) feed inputs into the same fixed-step sim the browser runs.
  - **Expert:** an oracle that presses at the ideal moment.
  - **Sloppy:** 40 ms late on average, ±85 ms timing jitter, misses 2% of hazards, makes 0.1 stray taps per second, switches color 150–350 ms after a gate becomes visible, and re-presses if a tap is swallowed mid-air.
  - **Very sloppy:** 60 ms late, ±120 ms jitter, misses 4% of hazards, makes 0.2 stray taps per second, and is slower in every reaction.
  - A sloppy bot aims its jump so the arc's apex sits over the middle of the hazard, which is how people judge it.
- **`npx tsx scripts/bot-report.mjs`** plays two cohorts:
  - 40 course seeds × 5 attempts each;
  - the shipped level (seed 7) × 200 different sloppy players × 5 attempts.
- **The browser harness** (`window.__harness`, or `?autoplay=good|sloppy&skip=cue|vertical`) runs the same bots in the real page. `scripts/record-clips.mjs` steps it frame by frame, so clips are deterministic and smooth on any machine.
- **Same bots, old build.** For the before numbers, the old `core.ts` (commit `de40bfb`) was played by the same bots and the same 200 players on seed 7.

## Before and after, measured

| Shipped level (seed 7), 200 players × 5 attempts | Before (sloppy) | After (sloppy) | After (very sloppy) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Survive the first 10 s | 83% | **99%** | 93% |
| Survive the first 15 s | 74% | **96%** | 77% |
| Reach the vertical, per attempt | 70% | **95%** | 70% |
| Reach the vertical within 3 attempts | 98% | **100%** | 97% |
| Survive the first 5 s of the vertical | **6%** | **100%** | 100% |
| Clear the vertical | 1% | **90%** | 72% |
| Finish the level | 0% | **60%** | 15% |
| Median run | 21 s (30%) | 77 s (100%) | 47 s (57%) |

- **Before:** "vertical gate" was 696 of the sloppy bot's 1,000 deaths; the wall really was a death wall.
- **After:** sloppy deaths are spread across gaps, shaft gates, spikes and platforms after the shaft. That is where the level is meant to get hard.
- **The expert** clears both builds on every seed.

Across 40 random course seeds, the sloppy bot survives 10 s in 100% of runs and reaches the vertical within 3 attempts on every seed. Nobody dies in the first 5 s of the shaft. `tests/bots.test.js` locks these properties in CI.

A bot bug was found and fixed along the way:

- **The bug:** after a press was swallowed mid-air, the sloppy bot re-pressed as soon as it landed, not when the hazard was due. That made 49% of perfectly timed runs die at one platform at 56.9 s.
- **The fix:** the re-press is now timed to the hazard.

### Jump windows

Each window is the range of press times that clear a lone hazard at 240 units/s, in seconds before the runner's back edge reaches it. The same sweep was run on both builds.

| Hazard | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spike (34 high) | 0.110–0.555 (0.45 s) | 0.100–0.570 (0.48 s) |
| Double spike | 0.110–0.440 (0.34 s) | 0.100–0.455 (0.36 s) |
| Triple spike | 0.110–0.320 (0.22 s) | 0.100–0.340 (0.25 s) |
| 100-unit gap | −0.025–0.330 (0.36 s) | −0.050–0.330 (0.39 s) |
| Platform | 0.145–0.730 (0.59 s) | 0.145–0.730 (0.59 s) |

The windows were already reasonable and have grown only slightly: hitboxes are inset by 3 units, and a 0.1 s coyote time lets you jump just after leaving a gap's edge.

Most of the improvement in survival comes from four other changes:

- **A 0.15 s jump buffer.** A press just before landing is no longer swallowed.
- **What the opening asks for.** The first 20 s are single low spikes four beats apart, each with an orb on its arc, then one color gate, plus a free shield that forgives the first mistake. There are no gaps in the opening; before, a gap could appear at 4%.
- **More lookahead on phones.** See the framing table below.
- **A vertical section that can be learned.** See "The vertical, as a set piece".

## The vertical, as a set piece

| Beat | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| −5.0 s | A "GRAVITY TURNS AHEAD ▲" banner and a two-note cue sound. Generation holds, so the last 200 units before the portal are clear. |
| In view | A tall glowing portal with scrolling up-chevrons, and beat-lit chevrons on the runway pointing at it. It brightens as you approach. |
| 0 s | Whoosh. Slow-mo (0.42×, music pitch follows). The scene rotates a quarter turn around the runner, so the runway *becomes* the cyan left wall (0.8 s of game time, about 1.9 s real). |
| Enter | Flash. One line, the first time only: "TAP TO SWAP WALLS". A shield orb on the far wall rewards the first tap. |
| 0–5.1 s | Safe runway: nothing lethal before y = 900; speed ramps from 170 to 300 over 18 s. |
| Then | Nine gate rows, 2.4 s apart at first, never closer than 1.25 s, never the same side three times in a row. The far-wall shield breaks one row, and an orb waits on the open side past every row. |
| Exit | Reverse rotation back onto the runway, "NICE!", and gentle terrain lessons (a gap, a platform, a shield, a double spike) before the harder phrases. |

- **Portrait:** the lane fills the phone's width.
- **Landscape:** the lane is centered, with parallax neon towers on both sides; the progress readout moves beside the lane.

## Look

- **Backdrop:** a striped synthwave sun, stars, mountains and a skyline with parallax. Three sky palettes (dusk, ember, lagoon) crossfade every section.
- **Floor:** a perspective grid that scrolls 1:1 with the runway and pulses on the beat at 115 BPM.
- **Hazards:** gradient spikes with white cores; gates solid when lethal and dashed when passable. Gaps are black with red edges; danger rows in the shaft are hatched.
- **Effects:** glow on every hazard, cached so it stays cheap, plus an adaptive bloom pass. The runner has a ribbon trail.
- **Feedback:** particles on jump, landing, orbs, color swaps and death. Shake and flash, a death shatter, PERFECT and streak popups, haptics.
- **Sound:** synthesized sound effects over the music track.

## Text and UI

- **Menu:** logo, PLAY, three mode chips (LEVEL / ENDLESS / PRACTICE), and one hint line.
- **Teaching by doing:** the first time a mechanic approaches, one short prompt appears ("TAP TO JUMP", "TAP ◈ TO SWITCH COLOR", "JUMP THE GAP", "LAND ON TOP", "JUMP THE PULSE"). The game briefly slows for spikes and gates while it is up.
- **Result card:** attempt, a big %, best or NEW BEST!, a big RETRY and a small MENU. Tapping anywhere retries; the restart takes well under 0.5 s.
- **HUD:** a progress bar with a best marker, the %, and pause.
- **Practice:** drops checkpoints automatically every 2.5 s of safe ground, and respawns instantly.
- **Layout tests:** `layout-tests/` checks at 360×640, 390×844, 430×932, 844×390, 932×430 and 1280×800 that no HUD element, prompt, banner or touch button overlaps, including during the approach banner and the shaft tutorial.

## Performance

`npm run perf` runs at 390×844 with DPR 3 and 4× CPU throttling, in headless software rendering:

- **Before:** median frame 16.7 ms. The old renderer drew much less.
- **After:** median frame 24.2 ms. Bloom switches itself off during the title demo on a device this slow.

Profiling with forced flushes found two costs:

- **The floor grid:** a gradient-stroked grid cost about 12 ms at DPR 2. It is now drawn in four solid depth bands and costs about 2 ms.
- **The shaft background:** a per-frame full-screen gradient, now a cached blit.

On GPU-backed phone canvases, bloom and the blits are cheap.
